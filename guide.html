How to Choose a Licensed Community Residential Setting in Minnesota

A Community Residential Setting (CRS) is a residential program — commonly a group home, foster care home, or supported living site — licensed by the Minnesota Department of Human Services (DHS) under Minnesota Statutes Chapter 245D, the state's Home and Community-Based Services standards. This guide explains what that licensing actually covers, how the service categories differ, and what's worth asking before you or someone you're helping moves forward — using the same 945-location, 67-county dataset that powers this directory's search.

What is a Community Residential Setting (CRS)?

A CRS is a licensed residential program where DHS has approved the operator to provide 24-hour or scheduled support to people with disabilities, mental illness, or other qualifying needs, outside of a hospital or institutional setting. Licensing falls under Chapter 245D, and every CRS location holds a license number tied to a specific address and license holder (operator) — the same identifiers shown on every profile in this directory. DHS, not this site, is the licensing authority; this directory republishes DHS's own public data to make it searchable.

How do I check whether a provider's license is currently active?

Every profile in this directory shows a license-status badge pulled from the same DHS export: Active License, Provisional / Conditional, or Not Currently Licensed. Status can change between DHS data pulls, so for any time-sensitive placement decision, confirm current status directly with the provider or with DHS before referring or admitting someone — treat this directory as a fast, complete starting point, not a live regulatory feed.

What should I ask before choosing a provider?

  • Is the license currently Active, and does the service sub-type match the actual need (Foster Care vs. Crisis Respite vs. Adult Mental Health Certification, etc.)?
  • Does the location currently have openings, and how soon could someone be admitted?
  • What's the staff-to-resident ratio and on-site supervision schedule — ask directly, since DHS's public export doesn't include this level of operational detail?
  • Is the county and location practical for family visits, medical appointments, and case-manager check-ins?
  • Has the listing been claimed by the operator (a claimed profile can offer more detail than DHS's bare licensing record alone)?

How complete is this directory?

This prototype covers a 340-operator, 945-location launch cohort — every operator with fewer than 9 licensed locations statewide — not the full statewide list of Minnesota CRS providers.
